\subsection{The Source Metamodel}
\label{sec:sourcemetamodel}

The source metamodel is \texttt{model/project-intent.ecore} in the archive. A
\emph{project} document specifies application requirements. One \emph{platform}
document specifies cluster facts and policies (Section~\ref{sec:sharedvalues}).

\subsubsection{The Project Document}

An application using a database declares a migration. One application migrates
each project's database. \texttt{Migration} has three subclasses. The platform
applies a \texttt{ManagedMigration} changelog before the new version starts.
\texttt{SelfMigration} leaves schema changes to the image.
\texttt{NoMigration} leaves them to another application.

\texttt{cutover} specifies whether service continues during an upgrade. For
\texttt{continuous}, the new version receives traffic after the application's
processes pass metric checks. This is a \emph{blue/green} switch. For
\texttt{interrupted}, the old version stops first. \texttt{lifecycle} is
\texttt{application}, \texttt{job} or \texttt{prepare}. A \emph{prepare process}
performs one-off setup after migration and before the new version starts. It
serves no traffic.

An \texttt{Exposure} belongs to an application because its hostname can route
different paths to different processes. A \texttt{Surface} is a named port a
process offers. A route refers to a surface of its own application. A
dependency refers to a surface of another application.

\subsubsection{The Platform Document}

The platform document specifies shared cluster facts and policies. For example,
it states whether secrets are encrypted at rest (Section~\ref{sec:naming}). A
tier refers to the application that runs its proxy. The delivery policy refers
to the \emph{delivery machinery}: applications that switch other applications
to a new version. Constraints reject secret delivery without encryption and
storage requests without matching policies (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}).

\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.22\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.70\linewidth}}

\textbf{Class} & \textbf{What it represents} \\
\hline
Platform & One document per cluster, with its owner and container security restrictions. \\

Substrate & Cluster facts that other decisions depend on: its version, datastore, server count, whether secrets are encrypted at rest, and its network plugin and policy controller. \\
Bootstrap & What must exist before the first generated object can be applied. The document records it, and the generators do not create it. \\
FluxSource & The source reference of Flux, the tool that fetches the generated files and applies them to the cluster. \\
RenderedArtifacts & The registry repository each project's generated output is published to, one signed artifact per project. \\
ArtifactSigner & The keyless identity, an issuer and a subject, whose signature Flux accepts on a published artifact. \\
VaultState & Whether the secret store is unsealed, the second bootstrap fact. \\
Tier & An entry point of the edge: its audiences, listener, certificate source, authentication endpoint, and the declared Application that acts as its proxy. \\

EphemeralPolicy & The size a declared writable path receives. \\
Provider & A service the cluster runs that this model does not deploy, with its address and named surfaces, which a dependency may refer to. \\
MigrationPolicy & The runner image alias, deadline and resources for managed migrations. \\
DeliveryPolicy & The delivery machinery: the applications that switch other applications to a new version and are not switched themselves. \\
AnalysisPolicy & How often a new version is checked, how many passing checks promote it, and how many failing ones roll it back. \\
HandoverLedger & Projects on the old path and projects delivered from pinned artifacts. It also records the old path's removal date. \\
